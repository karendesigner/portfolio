Case study · IG Group
IG GroupFintechiOS · Android · Web

Investment App

Simplifying investing for new European markets, starting with France.

RoleSole Product Designer
ScopeResearch → UI → design system
Outcome1k users, first month

01The problem

IG Group wanted to bring its UK investment offering to Europe, starting with France. The UK app was built for traders: complex, full of jargon and intimidating for new investors. The challenge was a mobile-first app for iOS and Android, made for French investors.

One key constraint: technical dependencies meant the account page couldn't change. I had to design the rest of the app around it and still keep the experience cohesive.

Success metric: strong adoption, with 1k users in the first month.

02Competitor analysis

I started by analysing how other investment apps balance accessibility and advanced features. This gave me benchmarks for navigation patterns, dashboards and onboarding flows.

03Research and navigation

I ran discovery interviews with investors in France, Germany and Spain. People consistently said the UK app felt overwhelming, even "elitist". Their main needs were clear dashboards, simple onboarding, and transparency on costs and portfolio performance.

In response, I restructured navigation into one clear flow: login → overview → search → market details → deal ticket → review. The portfolio dashboard became the central hub. Proof-of-concept flows covering three levels of navigation guided the usability tests and validated the information hierarchy.

04User testing and validation

I ran usability sessions with French users of the IG UK app to validate flows before launch. I used Claude to analyse the qualitative feedback and surface recurring patterns, and Figma Make to build interactive prototypes faster. Together these shortened the step from raw feedback to the fixes below.

Issue observedImpact on usersIteration / fix
Euro symbol missingConfusion about pricingAdded the currency symbol consistently
Order expiry unclearUsers missed expiry detailsHighlighted order expiration in the deal flow
P&L timeframe not obviousMisunderstood performance figuresClarified timeframe labels in the portfolio
Account page limitationFelt inconsistent with the new UIDesigned surrounding flows to feel cohesive

05Results

The redesigned app launched successfully in France. The simpler onboarding and dashboard-first approach drove early adoption, and the app reached 1k users in its first month in a competitive market.

  • UX impact: a more intuitive, modern experience than the UK app.
  • Scalable foundation: the design system kept mobile and web consistent, ready for future EU rollouts.
  • Validation: MVP adoption confirmed product-market fit, with demand for fractional shares, research tools and tax wrappers.

07Design system

To make the product scalable, I created a design system with reusable tokens, variables and components. It supported both mobile and web, sped up delivery and kept the brand consistent for future European launches.

08Dark mode and accessibility

I added dark mode on the same token architecture, so colours adapt across themes without extra component variants. I also applied WCAG standards to colour contrast, typography and interaction states, so the system is accessible by default and needs less rework from designers and developers.

09Learnings

  • Localisation matters: research showed that reusing the UK app would have failed in France.
  • Balance simplicity and sophistication: investors valued clarity, but advanced users also needed depth.
  • Design drives adoption: the app's early growth showed how UX decisions shape business outcomes.
  • Future-ready: the groundwork is in place for fractional shares, automation, tax wrappers and broader instrument coverage.

Case study · Utility Warehouse
Utility WarehouseDesign systemiOS · Android · Web · Figma

Mobile-First DS

Building a scalable design system

01Situation

Utility Warehouse was preparing for a new visual identity, but their legacy design system was fragmented, inconsistent, and hard to maintain. Designers often recreated components from scratch, while developers lacked clear standards or documentation. Scaling design across mobile and web products had become inefficient.

This challenge presented an opportunity to rebuild the system from the ground up — with a mobile-first approach to support the rebrand and future product growth.

02My role

As the Senior Design System Designer, I led the initiative by:

  • Partnering with product designers to capture business and product needs.
  • Pairing with developers to translate components into code and document them in Storybook.
  • Defining naming conventions, foundations, and adoption strategies to ensure long-term scalability.

03Process

1. Auditing the legacy UI
I began with a comprehensive audit of components across products, surfacing redundancies and inconsistencies in typography, colour, spacing, and interactions.

To support this process, I also leveraged AI tools like GPT to analyse and compare usage across the Figma libraries. This allowed me to identify which components were most frequently used, which were redundant, and where gaps existed — helping me prioritise the highest-impact work for the first delivery phase.

2. Planning the system
Created a phased roadmap to prioritise delivery:

  • Phase 1: tokens, grids, spacing, typography, colours, buttons, inputs, modals.
  • Phase 2: navigation patterns, banners, illustration library, advanced components.

3. Defining foundations
Established design tokens for typography, colour, spacing, and motion. Accessibility was embedded into every layer — from semantic colour naming to scalable typography.

4. Component design & naming conventions
Designed reusable, flexible components with predictable naming conventions (e.g. Variant/ColourScheme/Size/State/Icon). This standardisation improved usability for designers and ensured consistency across Figma and Storybook.

5. Documentation & adoption

  • Embedded guidelines directly in Figma so designers could access them at the point of use.
  • Paired with engineers to implement components in Storybook with coded examples.
  • Ran workshops to onboard teams and drive adoption across design and development.

04Results

  • Established a unified set of design tokens for colour, typography, spacing, and motion.
  • Built a predictable component library with consistent naming conventions.
  • Embedded usage guidelines directly in Figma and documented coded assets in Storybook.
  • Defined a cross-platform release workflow, ensuring iOS, Android, Web, and Figma libraries stayed in sync.

05Dynamic component

To support a mobile-first approach, I designed components that adapt automatically to different breakpoints. Using Figma variables, components dynamically adjusted their properties according to the viewport — for example, a modal that shifts layout and spacing between mobile, tablet, and desktop.

This approach ensured consistency across devices while reducing the need for designers and developers to maintain multiple variants. It also future-proofed the system for scaling into new platforms with minimal extra effort.

06Dark mode & accessibility

Accessibility and inclusivity were core principles in building the system. I introduced dark mode support using the same token-based architecture, ensuring colours adapted seamlessly across themes without creating extra component variants.

In parallel, I applied WCAG standards to colour contrast, typography, and interaction states, making the system accessible by default. This not only improved usability for all users but also reduced rework for designers and developers by embedding accessibility directly into the foundations.

07Outcome

  • Efficiency: designers assembled pages in hours instead of days, reducing duplication and rework.
  • Consistency: reduced design and development inconsistencies across platforms.
  • Collaboration: improved alignment between design and engineering through shared standards and documentation.
  • Future-proofing: positioned Utility Warehouse with a scalable, mobile-first foundation for the upcoming rebrand.

SaaS Design System

At Sopra Banking Software, as a Product Designer, I was responsible for designing and managing a scalable design system tailored for the B2B platform.

01Overview

The goal was to address inconsistencies, improve efficiency, and create a unified experience for complex user workflows in a professional banking environment.

02Problem statement

The B2B platform lacked a cohesive design approach, leading to inefficiencies in development and inconsistent user experiences. As the platform grew, the need for a scalable, centralised system to streamline design and engineering collaboration became critical.

03Product audit

I began by conducting a comprehensive audit of the existing platform, cataloguing UI components, design patterns, and user workflows. Partnered with stakeholders and developers to understand their perspectives.

Modals are inconsistent throughout the platform; Patterns identified.

04Analysis

This audit highlighted redundancies and gaps, serving as the foundation for a streamlined design system. Industry analysis, best practice.

05Styles and variables

Standardised foundational design variables, including colour palettes, typography, and grid systems, to establish a cohesive visual framework.

06Components

Using the defined variables and styles, I designed modular, reusable components optimised for B2B workflows.

07Documentation

I developed documentation for the design system, detailing how components should be used.
I also encouraged team members to contribute by sharing their use cases or suggesting improvements.

08A11y

All components were created to meet WCAG 2.1 AA standards, ensuring sufficient colour contrast.

09Testing

Conducted usability testing with iterative feedback loops, continuously refining components and styles to ensure the system effectively addressed real-world user needs.

10Collaboration

I worked closely with developers to ensure smooth integration of the design system into the platform. Facilitated collaborative workshops and design reviews to align stakeholders and proactively address gaps during implementation.

11Success metrics

Detachment of design instances was reduced by 80%, significantly improving Figma design specification consistency and accelerating delivery timelines. Development time decreased due to well-prepared documentation aligned with developers, eliminating ambiguities and assumptions.

Enhancing Transaction Search

01Situation

I was responsible for the end-to-end design process of an audit around account and transaction features at Sopra Banking Software to identify opportunities for improvement. During this audit, I discovered that users were struggling to locate specific transactions and view their details, leading to frustration and inefficiency.

02Task

My task was to propose and design solutions that would simplify the process of finding and viewing transaction details. The goal was to improve user experience by making the process more intuitive and efficient.

03Action

I analyzed how other apps addressed similar issues and used those insights to propose two design solutions:

  • Full-page filter: Displayed all filter options on one page, providing a comprehensive view but overwhelming users with too much information.
  • Pill filters: Allowed users to filter results through clickable pills, offering a more streamlined, manageable approach.

I created high-fidelity prototypes for both options and conducted usability testing. During testing, it became clear that the full-page filter caused cognitive overload, while the pill filter offered a more intuitive and user-friendly experience.

05Results

The pill filter solution resulted in:

  • Faster task completion times.
  • Increased user satisfaction, as users found it easier to navigate and locate specific transactions.
  • A more intuitive and enjoyable user experience overall. The pill filter proved to be the more effective design solution, as confirmed by usability tests and positive user feedback.

06Learnings

Through this project, I learned the importance of simplifying user interactions. The contrast between the full-page filter and the pill filter reinforced how users value clarity over complexity. I also realised the significance of user testing in validating design decisions, as it highlighted the cognitive load caused by overwhelming options. This experience strengthened my ability to make design choices that prioritize user ease, and reinforced the need for continuous iteration to refine the user experience.

02Challenges
